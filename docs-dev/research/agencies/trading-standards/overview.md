## UK Trading Standards: Local Authority Enforcement and Prosecutions

### Overview of Trading Standards Enforcement

Most consumer protection legislation is enforced by the Trading Standards department of local authorities. Each council will maintain an investigations and enforcement policy in accordance with The Regulators' Code.(no content)Although there is a National Trading Standards Authority, investigations and prosecutions are led by local Trading Standards offices. Depending upon the facts of the case, there are a vast number of offences.

### Prosecution Powers and Penalties

Many breaches of Trading Standards law are criminal offences and can be prosecuted in the Magistrates' Court or Crown Court. A successful prosecution may have a range of consequences, including a punishment or sentence. Trading Standards offences can usually be punished with a fine, and in many cases the amount is unlimited. For the most serious cases, imprisonment is an option, with maximum periods of up to two years for some Trading Standards offences.(no content)Where a business is prosecuted for fraud, theft or money laundering, in addition to or instead of Trading Standards offences, or for offences under intellectual property law (trade marks and copyright), maximum penalties can be very high indeed (up to 14 years' imprisonment).

### Dangerous Goods Regulations

Offences under Regulation 5 of the General Product Safety Regulations 2005 apply to producers and distributors, including placing any product on the market unless it is safe, or supplying/possessing in order to supply a product that is known to be a dangerous product.(no content)Where products are dangerous, the enforcement authority may apply to the court for an order for their forfeiture and destruction. However, as an alternative to destruction, the court may permit the supply of the product to a person for repair or reconditioning or for scrap.(no content)Under section 20 of the regulations, a person, producer or distributor who is convicted of an offence could face up to 12 months imprisonment and up to a £20,000 fine.

### Recent Enforcement Successes

The importance of Trading Standards Authorities' contribution to public safety is demonstrated by Greenwich and Redbridge Trading Standards teams who, through Operation Altham and Operation Furlong, both seized sizable quantities of dangerous consumer electronic goods including phone chargers and batteries.(no content)Mr Reza Tahir Ahmed of Aberfan, Merthyr Tydfil was successfully prosecuted by Rhondda Cynon Taf Council's Trading Standards department. He pleaded guilty to 14 offences relating to the sale and possession for supply of counterfeit tobacco products, dangerous goods and running a fraudulent business.(no content)Trading Standards are not just seizing and prosecuting criminals, they are also going after the proceeds of crime recovering illegal profits for public benefit. Examples include cases in Bradford, Berkshire and Ceredigion. This coordinated approach demonstrates Trading Standards' commitment to upholding intellectual property laws while simultaneously protecting public safety.

### Other Enforcement Options

A Simple Caution is a formal warning, which may be offered as an alternative to prosecution, where it is in the public interest to do so. There is no obligation on Trading Standards services to offer a Simple Caution, but it might be offered for relatively minor first-time offending.(no content)For a range of breaches, Trading Standards services can apply to the County Court or High Court for an Enforcement Order requiring the business to comply with the law. A breach of the Order is contempt of court, which carries a maximum penalty of a fine and two years' imprisonment.
