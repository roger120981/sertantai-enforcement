## DVSA Overview

The DVSA carries out driving tests, approves people to be driving instructors and MOT testers, carries out tests to make sure lorries and buses are safe to drive, carries out roadside checks on drivers and vehicles, and monitors vehicle recalls.(no content)DVSA is an executive agency, sponsored by the Department for Transport.(no content)DSA and VOSA closed on 31 March 2014, and DVSA took over their responsibilities on 1 April 2014.

## Enforcement Powers

The DVSA enforcement sanctions policy sets out what action DVSA traffic examiners will take when dealing with most offences that they find when they carry out roadside checks. It's used to make sure that DVSA takes consistent action when carrying out checks.(no content)DVSA officers have broad powers to stop and inspect vehicles, enter premises under certain conditions, and demand documentation when carrying out investigations. Where issues of non-compliance are identified, the DVSA has various enforcement powers, including the ability to issue fixed penalties and prohibition notices. In some cases, enforcement by the DVSA can lead to prosecution in the criminal courts.

## Types of Investigations

DVSA investigations are often triggered following roadside inspections, routine vehicle and operator centre checks, or reports of non-compliance. There are three main types of investigations: Maintenance investigations (assessing whether vehicles are being kept in a roadworthy condition), Traffic investigations (examining drivers' hours, tachograph data, and load security), and Desk-based assessments (remote reviews of documentation and compliance records).

## Operator Compliance Risk Score (OCRS)

The DVSA uses the Operator Compliance Risk Score (OCRS) to assess and prioritise enforcement activity. This score is based on an operator's history of compliance, including roadside inspections, MOT results, and recorded offences.

## Prosecution Examples

On 12 February 2024, Peterborough Bazaar Ltd was found guilty of overload offences at Ipswich Magistrates Court. The prosecution followed a DVSA roadside check on 27 July 2023 which found an Iveco van bursting with food and drink on a journey from Peterborough to Ipswich. It weighed 5,610kg – more than 60% overweight! The company was told to pay £28,230 in fines and costs.

## Referral Process

If the DVSA suspect that an operator has broken conditions on their licence or committed some other form of transport offence, they can refer the company to the Traffic Commissioner. The Traffic Commissioner may then decide to convene a hearing to decide whether to take regulatory action.(no content)If DVSA establishes there is sufficient evidence that an offence has been committed, they may share personal data with the Police and Crown Prosecution Service (CPS), and with the Traffic Commissioners.
