## Main UK Health & Safety Regulatory Agencies

### Health and Safety Executive (HSE)
The start of 2025 marked the 50th anniversary of the Health and Safety Executive (HSE). HSE came into being through the Health and Safety at Work etc Act 1974, a hugely significant consolidation and improvement of existing safety standards.(no content)There have been many changes since HSE's creation, including new offences such as Corporate Manslaughter, the introduction of the Sentencing Guidelines, and shifts in industrial practices. Fines that used to be measured in hundreds might now stretch to millions. Directors that might once have received nominal fines now face up to 2 years in prison – longer in the case of manslaughter.

## Official Registers of Prosecutions and Enforcement

The HSE maintains several public registers:

The HSE registers contain enforcement information on notices and convictions for Great Britain. Information for Northern Ireland or the Republic of Ireland authorities is not available on this site.

### Available Registers:
- **Public register of convictions (up to 12 months old)** - provides details of prosecutions taken by HSE which resulted in a conviction.
- **Public register of convictions (between one and ten years old)** - provides details of prosecutions against organisations taken by HSE which resulted in a conviction.
- **Public register of enforcement notices (up to five years old)**(no content)The public register provides details of HSE prosecutions resulting in a successful conviction within the last 5 years. To account for the appeals process and internal quality assurance, cases are published 9 weeks following conviction.

## Local Authority Enforcement

The National Local Authority Enforcement Code encourages local authorities to publicise details of their health and safety intervention, enforcement and prosecution activity to encourage local accountability. LAs share this information with HSE to allow the preparation of national data.(no content)Local Authorities provide HSE with details of prosecutions taken under health and safety legislation via an on-line survey. This information is collated annually and contributes to the national enforcement statistics. HSE publishes details of prosecutions taken during the last 5 years providing a single data source for LAs to benchmark their activity.

## Key Statistics and Trends

Findings show that the Health and Safety at Work (HSW) Act accounted for 46% of all HSE prosecution cases in the last decade, representing nearly half of the total safety and health at work prosecutions.(no content)The available data reveal that 230 health and safety convictions were recorded for the construction industry alone in 2016. 34% of recorded offenses were committed under the HSW regulations, attracting total fines amounting to £6,181,060, with 19 prison sentences.(no content)SMEs are dominant entities in major industries such as construction, mining, etc; yet these categories of businesses are 10 times more likely to be prosecuted for breaching health and safety offenses.

## Recent High-Profile Fines

Last year, National Grid were fined £3.2M following a life-changing electrocution incident that occurred in December 2020, with another 8 companies facing fines above £1 million across the year.(no content)2023 saw extremely high fines for the likes of Network Rail, Morrisons, Serco and Transport for London.
