## ICO Data Protection Enforcement

On 16 September 2024, the UK's data protection authority, the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), issued a reprimand against Sky Betting and Gaming for unlawfully processing people's data through advertising cookies without their consent.(no content)Between 10 January and 3 March 2023, SkyBet's website dropped third-party AdTech cookies to visitors' browsers before visitors could accept or reject them via a cookie banner. As a result, the visitors' personal data was shared automatically with third-party AdTech companies without visitors' consent or a lawful basis.(no content)In 2024, there were a total of 62 instances of enforcement action (fines, reprimands and enforcement notices) taken against 47 organisations (including 8 police forces or other law enforcement agencies).(no content)A £750,000 penalty was issued to the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI) for a security breach described as "the most significant data breach… in the history of UK policing." Additionally, a £350,000 penalty was imposed on the Ministry of Defence and a £7,500 fine for the Central YMCA.(no content)The ICO has published new draft guidance about how it proposes to conduct data protection investigations and enforcement action. Organisations may be particularly interested in aspects of the draft guidance relating to the ICO's new investigative and enforcement powers introduced under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.

## Coordination Between Regulators

The ICO enforces the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR) which sits alongside the Data Protection Act. PECR contains specific rules on marketing calls, emails and texts, and the use of cookies (and similar technologies).(no content)The Gambling Commission works with the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) on data protection, the National Crime Agency (NCA) on illegal gambling networks, and NHS & Public Health England on gambling harm research and prevention. These partnerships address gambling's wider risks, from fraud to addiction and mental health.

## Ofcom Enforcement Powers

The ICO's power to require the appointment of an "approved person" is similar to powers available to the Financial Conduct Authority and Prudential Regulation Authority. A corresponding power is now also available to Ofcom under section 104 of the Online Safety Act.(no content)The ICO reports that the gambling sector is one of the most complained about sectors regarding direct marketing. The Gambling Commission and ICO are concerned about consumers receiving such direct marketing without their genuine consent.
