## UK Water Regulation and Enforcement

### Regulatory Structure

The regulation of water companies in the UK involves multiple agencies and regulatory bodies, each with distinct roles. Ofwat, the Environment Agency, the Drinking Water Inspectorate, and CCW work collectively to ensure water services are economically viable and environmentally sound.(no content)Ofwat was set up after the privatisation of the water sector to ensure that water companies in England and Wales cover their statutory functions.(no content)The Drinking Water Inspectorate monitors the quality of drinking water in England and Wales.

### Major Regulatory Overhaul Announced

A new regulator will take responsibility of water functions across Ofwat, Environment Agency, Natural England and Drinking Water Inspectorate. In the biggest overhaul of the water sector since privatisation, Ofwat will be abolished and its functions will be merged with water functions across these agencies to form a new single, powerful regulator.

### Enforcement Powers and Prosecutions

Provisions already exist to allow the Environment Agency, Natural Resources Wales and the Drinking Water Inspectorate (DWI) to bring criminal charges against companies and executives, but there is a high evidential bar that must be met.(no content)Since privatisation, only three water company officials have been criminally prosecuted for obstruction by the Environment Agency without appeal, and the maximum punishment was merely a fine – though no fines were issued.(no content)All three regulators can issue fines for non-compliance with regulations. Ofwat and the Environment Agency can issue fines directly. The Drinking Water Inspectorate can apply to the courts to impose fines.(no content)Ofwat can impose financial penalties of up to 10% of a company's relevant turnover. In deciding whether to impose a penalty and the level of that penalty, Ofwat will take account of the particular facts and circumstances of the case.

### Drinking Water Inspectorate Enforcement Policy

The decision on whether to commence a prosecution is taken by the Chief Inspector, independently of the lead inspector. In deciding whether to commence a prosecution, the Chief Inspector will have regard to the Code for Crown Prosecutors. The Chief Inspector will only commence a prosecution when satisfied that there is a realistic prospect of conviction on the available evidence.(no content)In addition to relevant matters set out in the Code for Crown Prosecutors, the Chief Inspector will consider factors including the impact or potential impact of the offence on consumers and the implications of the offence for the credibility and enforcement of the regulatory regime.

### Enforcement Records

Water companies operating in England have received over 800 enforcement actions since 2010.(no content)Southern Water has been fined over £220 million since 2010. It has been sanctioned eight times by the Drinking Water Inspectorate, 66 times by the Environment Agency and twice by Ofwat.(no content)Thames Water has been fined a total of £166.5 million. The firm has been sanctioned three times by the Drinking Water Inspectorate, 82 times by the Environment Agency and five times by Ofwat.

### Recent Legislative Changes

The Water (Special Measures) Act 2025 has been signed into law, marking the most significant increase in enforcement powers in a decade. The Act strengthens regulation to ensure water bosses face personal criminal liability for lawbreaking and gives the water regulator new powers to ban the payment of bonuses if environmental standards are not met.(no content)Under the Act, harsher penalties for law-breaking are introduced, including jail sentences of up to two years for executives who fail to cooperate or obstruct Environment Agency and Drinking Water Inspectorate investigations.
